Dot plots are among the oldest ways of looking at a genome: put two sequences on the two axes and draw a dot wherever they resemble each other. Stretches that stay in step form the diagonal, a stretch flipped end to end draws the anti-diagonal, and a stretch that moved somewhere else lands as a square off the axis. The picture offers no explanation, but it turns structure into something the eye can take in at once. This tool does not go beyond that. What it offers is nothing to install — no conda, no Java runtime, no handing eighty megabytes of FASTA to somebody else’s server. The files stay on your disk and the work happens on your CPU. What it is not It does not align base by base. Gaps between collinear blocks are treated as breakpoints, so what you see is structure, not single-base insertions and deletions. When you need an exact variant list, export the blocks and hand them to minimap2 or MUMmer. This tool is for looking first. Identity is inferred from seed density (p ≈ (seeds/span)^(1/k)); minimiser sampling bias makes it read slightly low, so treat it as an estimate.
